# Sāṃkhya

Sāṃkhya is one of the classical Indian philosophical traditions (āstika darśanas). It develops a systematic account of reality based on a fundamental distinction between **Puruṣa** and **Prakṛti**.

## Core question

How can conscious experience arise in a world of changing nature, and how can consciousness become free from suffering?

## Core claim

Classical Sāṃkhya holds that reality consists fundamentally of:

- **Puruṣa** — pure consciousness, the witnessing principle.
- **Prakṛti** — primordial nature, the source of the material and psychological world.

Puruṣa and Prakṛti are fundamentally distinct. Prakṛti undergoes transformation; Puruṣa does not.

## The 25 tattvas

Sāṃkhya presents reality through a structured enumeration of 25 principles (*tattvas*), including Prakṛti, Mahat/Buddhi, Ahaṃkāra, Manas, the senses, subtle elements, gross elements, and Puruṣa.

## Consciousness

For classical Sāṃkhya, consciousness belongs to Puruṣa rather than to the mind. Mind, intellect, ego, senses, and body are products of Prakṛti.

## The three guṇas

Prakṛti consists of three fundamental constituents:

- **Sattva** — clarity, lightness, harmony
- **Rajas** — activity, energy, movement
- **Tamas** — inertia, heaviness, obscuration

## Bondage and liberation

Bondage arises through confusion between Puruṣa and Prakṛti. Liberation (*kaivalya*) occurs through discriminative knowledge of their distinction.

## Epistemology

Classical Sāṃkhya accepts three principal means of valid knowledge (*pramāṇas*):

- **Pratyakṣa** — perception
- **Anumāna** — inference
- **Śabda** — reliable verbal testimony

## Relationship to Yoga

Classical Yoga shares much of Sāṃkhya's metaphysical framework, including Puruṣa, Prakṛti, and liberation through discriminative knowledge. Yoga, however, explicitly incorporates **Īśvara** and places distinctive emphasis on disciplined practice.

## Important thinkers and texts

- **Īśvarakṛṣṇa** — author traditionally associated with the *Sāṃkhya Kārikā*.
- **Vācaspati Miśra** — author of the influential *Tattva-kaumudī*.
- **Vijñānabhikṣu** — later influential interpreter of Sāṃkhya.

## Primary sources

- Īśvarakṛṣṇa — *Sāṃkhya Kārikā*
- *Tattva-kaumudī*
- Relevant passages in the *Bhagavad Gītā* and *Mahābhārata* should be treated carefully: their use of “Sāṃkhya” does not necessarily mean the fully developed classical system represented by Īśvarakṛṣṇa.

## Important distinction

Sāṃkhya is often classified as a form of **dualism**, but it should not be reduced to Western Cartesian dualism. The systems arise in different historical and conceptual contexts and make different claims about consciousness, matter, causation, and liberation.
