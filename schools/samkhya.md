# Sāṃkhya

**Type:** Indian philosophical school / darśana

Sāṃkhya is a classical Indian philosophical system centered on the distinction between **Puruṣa** (consciousness) and **Prakṛti** (primordial nature).

## Classification

Sāṃkhya is commonly classified as:

- **Dualistic** — Puruṣa and Prakṛti are fundamentally different.
- **Pluralistic with respect to Puruṣas** — classical Sāṃkhya generally accepts multiple Puruṣas.
- **Non-theistic in its classical form** — it does not require a creator God.
- **Liberation-oriented** — its metaphysics is connected to *kaivalya*, liberation.

These are analytical classifications, not substitutes for Sāṃkhya's own conceptual framework.

## Central distinction

> Puruṣa is conscious; Prakṛti is the source of the changing psychological and material world.

## Comparison

| Position | Consciousness | Matter / nature | Relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sāṃkhya | Puruṣa | Prakṛti | Fundamentally distinct |
| Advaita Vedānta | Ultimately Brahman | Not ultimately independent | Non-dual |
| Cartesian dualism | Mind/substance | Extended substance | Distinct substances |
| Physicalism | Ultimately physical | Physical reality | Consciousness depends on the physical |
| Panpsychism | Fundamental or ubiquitous | Fundamental | Mind-like properties are fundamental |

These comparisons are starting points, not claims of equivalence.

## Open problems

1. If Puruṣa is inactive and witnessing, how does it become associated with Prakṛti?
2. How can fundamentally different realities appear related?
3. Why are there multiple Puruṣas?
4. How does unconscious Prakṛti generate apparently purposeful psychological activity?
5. What exactly does discriminative knowledge change?
